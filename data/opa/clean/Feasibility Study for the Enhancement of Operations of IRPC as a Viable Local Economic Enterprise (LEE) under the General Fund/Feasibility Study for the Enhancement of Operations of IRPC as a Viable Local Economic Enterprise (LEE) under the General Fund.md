## Feasibility Study for the Enhancement of Operations of IRPC as a Viable Local Economic Enterprise (LEE) under the General Fund

## Historical Background

The Iloilo Rice Processing Complex, located in Barangay Amamaros, Pototan, Iloilo, officially started its operations on March 16, 2012, through a ₱196.36 million grant from South Korea. The state-of-the-art facility was established through a bilateral partnership among the Korea International Cooperation Agency (KOICA), the Philippine Department of Agriculture (DA), and the Local Government Units (LGUs) of Iloilo and Pototan, and was subsequently turned over to be managed and operated by the Pototan Farmers Multi-Purpose Cooperative (PFMPC). It was designed to mill up to 50 tons of palay per day and has a storage capacity of 1,100 tons and serve as a modern post-harvest facility to improve the efficiency of rice production, milling, drying, and storage, thereby reducing post-harvest losses and enhancing rice quality. As a major post-harvest asset, the primary objectives of the complex are to significantly reduce grain wastage by replacing traditional methods with modern mechanical drying and automated milling facilities, while enhancing the overall milling recovery rate and quality of local rice to make it highly competitive in wholesale markets. Furthermore, the complex aims to increase local farmers' incomes and eliminate their reliance on middlemen by providing affordable, localized cost-based custom services, including custom milling, mechanical drying, warehousing, and trucking support, ensuring farming groups retain maximum profit from their crop yields.

The provincial government has taken over the facility and intends to take the lead in its operations aligning with the hybrid rice program of the province by converting the facility into a sustainable local economic enterprise managed, supervised and controlled by the Iloilo Provincial Government. The key shifts made by the government in taking over focused on the following areas:

-  Governance: Transitioning from DA oversight to PLGU management.
-  Physical Infrastructure: Assessment, rehabilitation and expansion of facilities.
-  Financial Management Strategy: From cooperative-led procurement to government-backed service optimization and financing.
-  Operational Continuity: Reorganizing the operating personnel to assure continuity.

The previous years after the takeover marked the transition and stabilization efforts of the government focused on steadying the operations coupled with infrastructure upgrading and securing funds for infusion in its working capital towards sustainable operations aligned with the PLGUs Key Programs and economic development.

Currently, steps are taken on preparing a feasibility study and business plan to fully operationalize the RPC. ## Nature and Purpose

The Iloilo Rice Processing Complex is contemplated to be a Local Economic Enterprise (LEE) operated by the Iloilo Provincial Government as a strategic tool to provide the essential staple food of the province and fundamental services needed to enhance local economic growth while ensuring that operations are sustainable, accountable, and aligned with community needs.It is envisioned to be a revenue-generating business or service to provide the essential goods (rice) or services (rice value-adding), promote local development in the Iloilo Province and islands of Panay and Guimaras (Region 6) , and achieve financial self-sufficiency and economic stability in rice production.

It is intended to generate income while delivering rice at the barest minimum price and services which are cost-based that meet local community needs. This strategic action of the Iloilo provincial government aims to support local economic development, improve public service delivery, and contribute to poverty reduction by providing equitable access to essential goods and services.

## Benefits to the Iloilo Provincial Government (LGU)

The benefits of establishing the IRPC as a Local Economic Enterprise (LEE) are multifaceted and contribute significantly to the overall economic and social well-being of Iloilo Province, Region 6 and on a greater scale, to national development. These benefits highlight the importance of establishing a Local Economic Enterprise in creating a resilient and sustainable local economy in the province and region, benefiting both the community and the environment.

-  Revenue Generation: The Iloilo Rice Processing Complex (IRPC) as an LEE reduces the need for government subsidies and contribute to LGU finances since it will be generating its own income to cover its expenses;
-  Quality Rice Production and Improved Value adding Services: It will ensure the quality rice production at the least cost and equitable delivery of rice-value adding services to the constituents;
-  Economic Development: It will stimulate local economic growth in Iloilo Province and the entire Region 6 by attracting local investors and supporting SMEs;
-  Good Governance: It showcases and promotes good governance in both the LGU and the private sector by encouraging transparency, accountability, and citizen participation in local development;
-  Job Creation: To a certain extent the IRPC spurs job creation by providing employment opportunities for local residents and contributing to the overall job market;
-  Community Pride: It fosters a sense of community pride and identity, as they recognize and are acknowledged to be the backbone of the local economy and contribute to its growth and development.
-  Sustainability: It promotes sustainable practices in overall Rice Production and Value Chain, reducing reliance on long-distance transportation and minimizing carbon emissions which aligns with the goals of sustainable development.
-  Resilience and Ecological Wellbeing: The establishment of the IRPC as a Local Economic Enterprise lays down the foundation for systemic sustainability, which fosters community wealth, ecological health, and resilience through localized, regenerative economic activity within the Province of Iloilo and the whole Region 6.
-  Empowerment: The presence of IRPC empowers local entrepreneurs and businesses in the rice production industry as it fosters service innovation and serving as an "incubator" that should be replicated in other municipalities and provinces.

As sanctioned by the LGC, the structure and operating mechanism may include the establishment and operation of local economic enterprises (LEEs).

## Market Study

The Iloilo Rice Processing Complex (IRPC) represents a pivotal agricultural infrastructure initiative designed to modernize grain post-harvest operations, strengthen food security, and enhance farmgate value addition in Western Visayas. Traditionally, regional smallholder rice farmers face severe postharvest losses, grain degradation during wet-season harvesting, and restricted market access due to inadequate drying and milling facilities. The IRPC directly addresses these systemic vulnerabilities by providing an integrated, state-of-the-art facility offering mechanical grain drying, precision rice milling, commercial grain procurement, and logistics support. Operating as an agroindustrial service hub, the project bridges the gap between smallholder producers and commercial wholesale markets, transforming raw paddy into high-grade commercial rice while unlocking significant regional economic value.

Major sources of revenue come from the following products/services:

1. Commercial Rice Buy-and-Sell Operations (46.0)
2. Fee-for-Service Operations (53.5%)
3. Ancillary Logistics Services -Warehousing and Hauling (0.5%)

## Description of Products and Services:

- a. Milled rice - IRPC will buy wet paddy and process them to produce milled rice. Milled Rice has had its husk, bran, and germ removed, leaving only the white starchy endosperm produced by removing the outer layers of brown rice, including the husk, bran, and germ, through a milling process. This process results in a softer, fluffier grain with a milder flavor and longer shelf life compared to brown rice.

Types of Milled Rice

-  Polished Milled Rice: Fully milled and polished to give a smooth, shiny surface; often enriched with vitamins to compensate for nutrient loss.
-  Semi-Milled or Half-Milled Rice: Partially milled, retaining some of the bran and germ, offering more nutrients than fully milled rice.
-  Head Rice and Broken Rice: Milled rice can include whole grains (head rice) and broken pieces, which are graded for quality.
- b. By-Products (Rice Husks, Bran, Sorter and Brewer)
-  Rice husks or hulls are generated during the first stage of rice milling, when rough rice or paddy rice is husked. Used as fuel, bedding, or in building materials
-  Rice bran is generated when brown rice moves through the whiteners and polishers. Rich in nutrients, used for oil extraction or animal feed.
-  Broken rice/ Sorter: Sold for processed foods or feed
-  Brewer's rice is separated produced when milled rice is sifted.
- c. Custom Milling Services

IRPC will provide Custom milling services to farmers and paddy/rice producers using Integrated multi-stage processing facilities which uses sequential machines for cleaning, hulling, whitening, and polishing, minimizing mechanical stress and producing high-quality rice suitable for domestic and export markets. The process will convert harvested paddy into polished, edible rice by removing the husk, bran layers, and impurities while maximizing yield and quality. It transforms raw paddy into white rice suitable for consumption. The process involves cleaning, hulling, separating, whitening, and polishing the grains, ensuring that the rice is free from impurities and has minimal broken kernels.

-  Cleaning of Paddy

Raw paddy contains straw, dust, stones, and other debris. Precleaning uses rotary screens, air blowers, and sieves to remove these impurities, preventing damage to milling machinery and improving final rice quality

-  Husking / De-husking

The husk (outer protective layer) is removed using hullers or rubber roller machines, producing brown rice. Brown rice still contains the bran layer and endosperm. Proper husking minimizes grain breakage and preserves the kernel.

-  Paddy Separation

After husking, machines separate the brown rice from the husk. This ensures that only edible grains proceed to the next stage, while husks are collected as by-products for fuel or animal feed

-  Polishing / Whitening

Brown rice is passed through polishing machines to remove the bran layer, producing shiny, white rice. The degree of polishing can vary depending on consumer preference or regional standards. Polishing improves appearance, texture, and shelf life

-  Grading and Sorting

Rice is sorted by size, length, and broken grains using sieves and optical sorters. This step ensures uniformity and enhances market value. Broken rice is often used for processed foods or animal feed

-  Packaging

The final rice is weighed, blended if necessary, and packed for distribution. Proper packaging maintains quality and prevents contamination

## d. Custom Drying

Rice custom drying is the process of reducing moisture content for safe storage or improve texture after harvesting. Proper post-harvest drying maintains rice quality, reduces storage losses, and allows flexible marketing and milling.

Rice can contain up to 25% moisture upon harvest which is too high for safe storage. High moisture can lead to mold growth, discoloration, pest infestation, and reduced germination of seeds. The goal of postharvest drying is to reduce moisture to about 12%, which preserves quality and maximizes head rice yield. IRPC uses controlled high temperatures for rapid drying, suitable for commercial operations. Drying stops when the desired moisture is reached.

## e. Ancillary Services (Trucking/Warehousing)

Ancillary services such as Trucking, hauling and warehousing are supporting services that enhance the functionality, reliability and efficiency that complement the Custom Milling and Custom Drying services and which add value to IRPC services

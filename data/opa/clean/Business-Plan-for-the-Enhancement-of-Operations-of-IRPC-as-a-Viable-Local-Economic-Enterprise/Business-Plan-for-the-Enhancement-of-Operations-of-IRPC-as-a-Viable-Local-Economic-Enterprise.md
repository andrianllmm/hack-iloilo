## Business Plan for the Operations of IRPC as a Viable Local Economic Enterprise (LEE) under the General Fund

## I. Historical Background

The Iloilo Rice Processing Complex in Barangay Amamaros, Pototan, Iloilo, began operations on March 16, 2012, through a ₱196.36 million grant from South Korea. Developed through a bilateral partnership among the Korea International Cooperation Agency (KOICA), the Philippine Department of Agriculture (DA), and the local governments of Iloilo and Pototan, the facility was later turned over to the Pototan Farmers Multi-Purpose Cooperative (PFMPC) for management and operation. Designed to mill up to 50 tons of palay per day and store 1,100 tons, it provides modern drying, milling, storage, and related post-harvest services that improve efficiency, reduce losses, and enhance rice quality. By replacing traditional practices with mechanical drying and automated milling, the complex aims to reduce grain wastage and improve milling recovery, making local rice more competitive in wholesale markets. It also seeks to raise farmers' incomes and lessen reliance on middlemen by offering affordable, cost-based services such as custom milling, mechanical drying, warehousing, and trucking, enabling farming groups to retain more of the value of their harvests.

## II. Current Status

## Provincial Takeover and Strategic Direction

The Iloilo Provincial Government has assumed control of the facility and intends to lead its operations in alignment with the province's hybrid rice program. The facility will be developed as a sustainable local economic enterprise managed, supervised, and controlled by the provincial government. The transition focuses on four key areas:

- Governance: Transfer oversight from the Department of Agriculture to the Provincial Local Government Unit (PLGU).
- Physical Infrastructure: Assess, rehabilitate, and expand the facilities.
- Financial Management: Shift from cooperative-led procurement to government-backed financing and service optimization.
- Operational Continuity: Reorganize personnel to maintain uninterrupted operations.

## Transition and Stabilization

In the years following the takeover, the provincial government focused on stabilizing operations, upgrading infrastructure, and securing additional working capital. These efforts support sustainable operations aligned with the PLGU's key programs and broader economic development goals.

## III. Local Economic Enterprise: Nature and Purpose

The Iloilo Rice Processing Complex is envisioned as a Local Economic Enterprise (LEE) operated by the Iloilo Provincial Government. It will serve as a strategic mechanism for supplying the province's essential staple food, delivering services that support local economic growth, and ensuring operations remain sustainable, accountable, and responsive to community needs. As a revenue-generating enterprise, the IRPC will provide rice and value-adding services, promote development across Iloilo Province and the islands of Panay and Guimaras in Region VI, and pursue financial selfsufficiency and stability in rice production.

The enterprise will generate income while offering rice at the lowest feasible price and delivering cost-based services that address local needs. Through this initiative, the Iloilo Provincial Government seeks to advance local economic development, improve public service delivery, reduce poverty, and expand equitable access to essential goods and services.

## Benefits to the Iloilo Provincial Government (LGU)

Establishing the IRPC as a Local Economic Enterprise offers broad economic, social, and environmental benefits to Iloilo Province, Region VI, and the country. These benefits demonstrate how the enterprise can help build a more resilient, inclusive, and sustainable local economy.

- Revenue Generation: By generating sufficient income to cover operating expenses, the IRPC can reduce its dependence on government subsidies and strengthen LGU finances.
- Quality Rice Production and Improved Value-Adding Services: The enterprise will support cost-efficient production of quality rice and the equitable delivery of rice value-adding services to constituents.
- Economic Development: The IRPC can stimulate economic growth in Iloilo Province and across Region VI by attracting local investment and supporting small and medium enterprises.
- Good Governance: The enterprise can model good governance in both the public and private sectors by promoting transparency, accountability, and citizen participation in local development.
- Job Creation: The IRPC can create employment opportunities for local residents and contribute to a stronger regional labor market.
- Community Pride: By recognizing local producers and communities as vital contributors to the economy, the enterprise can strengthen local identity and pride.
- Sustainability: The IRPC will promote sustainable practices across the rice production value chain, reduce dependence on long-distance transport, and help lower carbon emissions in support of sustainable development goals.
- Resilience and Ecological Well-Being: Establishing the IRPC as an LEE will provide a foundation for systemic sustainability by building community wealth, supporting ecological health, and strengthening resilience through localized and regenerative economic activity in Iloilo Province and Region VI.
- Empowerment: The IRPC will empower local entrepreneurs and rice-industry businesses by encouraging service innovation and serving as a replicable incubation model for other municipalities and provinces.

## IV. Policy Framework: Organizational and Legal Basis 1

1. Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the Local Government Code of 1991 (LGC), mandates, as a primary operative principle of decentralization, the establishment of an accountable, efficient, and dynamic organizational structure and operating mechanism in the local government units (LGUs) that will meet the priority needs and service requirements of its communities. As sanctioned by the LGC, the structure and operating mechanism may include the establishment and operation of local economic enterprises (LEEs).

2. The policy framework that serves as basis for the creation and operation of LEEs is primarily the LGC including related circulars intended to strengthen compliance to the relevant provisions of the LGC and related national laws. The policies that guide the creation and operation of LEEs are:
- a. Section 3(b) emphasizes the responsibilities of LGUs to set up appropriate service delivery structures and mechanisms to meet the basic needs of its constituents, to quote: "Section 3(b). "There shall be established in every local government unit an accountable, efficient, and dynamic organizational structure and operating mechanism that will meet the priority needs and service requirements of its communities."
- b. Section 3(f) provides for inter-LGU partnerships in the provision of services and facilities including joint ventures in LEEs, to quote: "Section 3(f). Local government units may group themselves, consolidate or coordinate their efforts, services, and resources commonly beneficial to them."

## The Business Plan

## A. The Marketing Plan

## I. Description of Products and Services

Major sources of revenue based on past performance of the IRPC will come from the following products/services:

## 1. Commercial Rice Buy-and-Sell Operations (46.0%)

- a. Milled rice - IRPC will buy wet paddy and process them to produce milled rice. Milled Rice has had its husk, bran, and germ removed, leaving only the white starchy endosperm produced by removing the outer layers of brown rice, including the husk, bran, and germ, through a milling process.

Types of Milled Rice to be presented to the market:

- Polished Milled Rice
- Semi-Milled or Half-Milled Rice
- Head Rice and Broken Rice

## b. By-Products (Rice Husks, Bran, Sorter and Brewer)

- Rice husks or hulls are generated during the first stage of rice milling, when rough rice or paddy rice is husked. Used as fuel, bedding, or in building materials
- Rice bran is generated when brown rice moves through the whiteners and polishers. Rich in nutrients, used for oil extraction or animal feed.
- Broken rice/ Sorter: Sold for processed foods or feed
- Brewer's rice is separated produced when milled rice is sifted.

## 2. Fee-for- Custom Milling Service Operations (53.5%)

IRPC will provide Custom milling services to farmers and paddy/rice producers using Integrated multi-stage processing facilities which uses sequential machines for cleaning, hulling, whitening, and polishing, minimizing mechanical stress and producing high-quality rice suitable for domestic and export markets. The process will convert harvested paddy into polished, edible rice by removing the husk, bran layers, and impurities while maximizing yield and quality. It transforms raw paddy into white rice suitable for consumption. The process involves cleaning, hulling, separating, whitening, and polishing the grains, ensuring that the rice is free from impurities and has minimal broken kernels.

- Cleaning of Paddy

Raw paddy contains straw, dust, stones, and other debris. Precleaning uses rotary screens, air blowers, and sieves to remove these impurities, preventing damage to milling machinery and improving final rice quality

- Husking / De-husking

The husk (outer protective layer) is removed using hullers or rubber roller machines, producing brown rice. Brown rice still contains the bran layer and endosperm. Proper husking minimizes grain breakage and preserves the kernel.

- Paddy Separation

After husking, machines separate the brown rice from the husk. This ensures that only edible grains proceed to the next stage, while husks are collected as by-products for fuel or animal feed

- Polishing / Whitening

Brown rice is passed through polishing machines to remove the bran layer, producing shiny, white rice. The degree of polishing can vary depending on consumer preference or regional standards. Polishing improves appearance, texture, and shelf life

- Grading and Sorting

Rice is sorted by size, length, and broken grains using sieves and optical sorters. This step ensures uniformity and enhances market value. Broken rice is often used for processed foods or animal feed

- Packaging

The final rice is weighed, blended if necessary, and packed for distribution. Proper packaging maintains quality and prevents contamination

- Custom Drying

IRPC uses controlled high temperatures for rapid drying suitable for commercial operations. Drying stops when the desired moisture is reached. The goal of post-harvest drying is to reduce moisture to about 12%, which preserves quality and maximizes head rice yield for the farmer-customer.

## 3. Ancillary Logistics Services -Warehousing and Hauling (0.5%)

Ancillary services such as Trucking, hauling and warehousing are supporting services that enhance the functionality, reliability and efficiency that complement the Custom Milling and Custom Drying services, and which add value to IRPC major services.

## II. Supply and Demand Situation

## Rice Production Data for Western Visayas

According to 2023 data from the Philippine Statistics Authority, Western Visayas (Region VI) ranked as the country's third-largest rice-producing region. Data covering the 10-year period from 2013 to 2023 shows annual production ranging from 1.3 million to 3.02 million metric tons.

A Philippine Statistics Authority longitudinal study covering 1970 to 2025 shows an upward trend in annual palay (wet paddy) production in Western Visayas, reflecting sustained efforts to increase the region's total output.

## Rice Production (Supply)

In the first semester of the current year 2026, the rice industry in Western Visayas recorded an average Palay Production of 554 thousand metric tons valued at PhP29.81 billion pesos. This is with the exclusion of Negros Occidental which has

become part of the newly formed Negros Island Region (NIR). There is a total of 327,842 recorded rice farmers to produce this output in a total area of 197,971 hectares both irrigated and non-irrigated producing an average yield of 2.8 metric tons per hectare. Iloilo, Antique and Guimaras Island Provinces produce an average yield of between 3 to 4 metric tons per hectare and the other provinces in the region produce an average yield of less than 3 metric tons per hectare.

For Iloilo Province the Rice Industry has decreased remarkably from its record high of 837,852 metric tons of wet paddy (palay) in the year 2025 to only 257,686 metric tons in the first semester of 2026.

## Projections on the initial year of operations

The financial engine of the IRPC is built upon a hybrid, multi-stream revenue model that balances commercial commodity trading with high-margin, fee-for-service operations. Under full operational capacity, total annual revenue is projected at ₱46,308,630.00.

Fee-for-Service Operations (53.5% / ₱24,768,000.00): The primary cash flow stabilizer relies on custom drying and custom milling. Mechanical drying utilizes 5 drying bins operating over 180 annual days (processing 14,400,000 kg of wet paddy at ₱1.25/kg) to generate ₱14,400,000.00. Custom milling processes 5,760,000 kg of dried paddy (at

₱3.00/kg) to generate ₱10,368,000.00. Commercial Rice Buy-and-Sell Operations (46.0% / ₱21,292,950.00): Sourcing 833,333.33 kg of wet paddy using ₱15,000,000.00 in revolving working capital yields 400,000 kg of milled rice sold at an average wholesale price of ₱50.70/kg (₱20,279,000.00) alongside milling by-products (₱1,013,950.00). Ancillary Logistics Services (0.5% / ₱247,680.00): Dedicated hauling and warehousing services maximize fleet utilization.

## Analysis

Market and Strategic Position. The IRPC operates within a region characterized by substantial rice production, high per-capita consumption, and continuing demand for post-harvest services. Western Visayas' position as a major rice-producing region, together with the IRPC's established network of farmers, cooperatives, retailers, wholesalers, and institutional buyers, provides a credible market base. Although other milling providers operate in the region, the IRPC can differentiate itself through an integrated service platform that combines drying, milling, storage, hauling, rice trading, and by-product recovery under provincial oversight.

Operational and Financial Viability. The proposed hybrid revenue model balances fee-based services with commercial rice trading and ancillary income. Fee-forservice operations provide recurring cash flow, while rice trading and by-product sales create additional earning capacity. At normal capacity, projected first-year revenue of ₱46.31 million, a net operating surplus of ₱13.00 million, and a 28.08% net margin indicate strong potential viability. The projected 3.45% annual revenue growth from 2027 to 2031 further supports long-term sustainability, provided that utilization targets, procurement volumes, service demand, and cost assumptions are achieved.

Public Value and Development Impact. Provincial operation of the IRPC can convert commercial performance into measurable public benefits. Affordable postharvest services can reduce losses, improve milling recovery, and help farmers retain a greater share of the value of their harvests. Reliable rice supply and the allocation of earnings to the subsidized rice program can improve food access for eligible beneficiaries. The enterprise can also generate employment, support cooperatives and small businesses, strengthen local supply chains, and retain more economic activity within Iloilo. Its value should therefore be assessed through both financial results and public-service outcomes.

Key Risks and Management Requirements. The projections remain sensitive to palay prices, energy costs, plant utilization, equipment reliability, working-capital turnover, market competition, and the ability to collect revenues efficiently. Public ownership also requires clear decision rights, transparent procurement, disciplined cash management, regular maintenance, and timely internal and government audits. The proposed ₱15 million capitalization should therefore be accompanied by measurable targets for throughput, service affordability, farmer reach, operating margins, rice availability, employment, and subsidy beneficiaries. Regular performance reviews will be essential to determine whether the IRPC is simultaneously meeting its commercial, social, and governance objectives.

## Summary

This business plan proposes the Iloilo Rice Processing Complex (IRPC) as a viable Local Economic Enterprise operated by the Iloilo Provincial Government to deliver direct and lasting benefits to the public. Through government-managed drying, milling, storage, hauling, and rice-trading services, the IRPC can improve access to quality and reasonably priced rice, reduce post-harvest losses, and provide farmers and cooperatives with reliable, affordable services. Provincial oversight will also enable the enterprise to align its operations with food-security objectives, publicservice priorities, and the needs of communities across Iloilo.

The proposed operating model combines fee-based custom drying and milling, commercial rice trading, by-product sales, and ancillary logistics services. With ₱15 million in proposed working capital and operations at normal capacity, the IRPC is projected to generate first-year revenue of ₱46.31 million, a net operating surplus of ₱13.00 million, and a net margin of 28.08%, with revenue forecast to grow by 3.45% annually from 2027 to 2031. These earnings can reduce reliance on continuing government subsidy, finance improvements in service quality and capacity, and support the Provincial Government's subsidized rice program for eligible beneficiaries. Reinvestment in the enterprise can further expand farmer access, strengthen local supply chains, create employment, and keep more economic value within the province.

Establishing the IRPC as a provincially operated Local Economic Enterprise therefore offers benefits beyond financial sustainability. It can strengthen food security, protect farmer incomes, improve the availability and affordability of rice, generate local employment, support cooperatives and small businesses, and enhance the Provincial Government's capacity to deliver responsive public services. These outcomes will depend on adequate capitalization, a clearly defined governance and organizational framework, prudent financial management, reliable plant operations, sustained market development, transparent performance monitoring, and consistent compliance with internal and government audit requirements.

## Conclusion and Recommendations

The forgoing business plan confirms that operating the IRPC as a Local Economic Enterprise of the Iloilo Provincial Government is operationally feasible, commercially viable, and capable of delivering sustained public value. Beyond generating an operating surplus, the enhanced model can strengthen food security, improve access to quality and reasonably priced rice, reduce post-harvest losses, protect farmer incomes, support cooperatives and small businesses, create local employment, and expand the Provincial Government's capacity to provide responsive public services. To achieve these outcomes, the following actions are recommended:

1. Ensure adequate capitalization for reliable public service. Approve the proposed capital infusion to expand rice procurement, improve facility utilization, stabilize operations, and sustain the delivery of affordable drying, milling, storage, hauling, and rice-distribution services.
2. Establish accountable provincial governance and management. Clearly define roles, staffing requirements, decision-making authority, service standards, and performance accountability to ensure efficient, transparent, and uninterrupted operations aligned with provincial food-security and public-service priorities.
3. Prioritize farmer, cooperative, and consumer access. Strengthen partnerships with farmers, cooperatives, wholesalers, retailers, and institutional buyers; maintain fair and affordable service charges; and promote reliable access to quality rice and integrated post-harvest services across Iloilo.
4. Direct earnings toward sustainability and public benefit. Implement disciplined budgeting, cash management, cost monitoring, and revenue collection, and adopt clear reinvestment and cross-subsidy policies to improve service quality and capacity, support the Provincial Government's subsidized rice program, and reduce dependence on continuing subsidy.
5. Institutionalize transparent oversight and public-value monitoring. Conduct regular internal and government audits and periodic operational, financial, and social-impact reviews. Monitor service affordability, farmer reach, rice availability, employment generation, and beneficiary support alongside financial performance.
6. Adopt and resource the business plan beginning in 2027. Secure the required budget and establish measurable operational, financial, and publicbenefit targets. Review performance regularly to maintain financial sustainability, protect public resources, and keep the enterprise aligned with provincial priorities for food security, farmer welfare, employment, and inclusive local economic development.
